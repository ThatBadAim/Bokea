Three states, never colour alone

rail + shape + word
Late Was Red / Overdue. Now a 4px rail and one word — the wash is gone, and no screen counts the days.
Due today Was Amber / Due Soon. A clock face, so it reads at a glance in greyscale and for red-green colour blindness.
Safe Was Green. Filed under Ticking along and kept off the Today screen entirely — safe things do not need looking at.
Health Money People Mind Life areas are told apart by icon and word only — colour stays reserved for urgency.

One filled button per screen. Everything competing with it is outlined, grey, and smaller.

Three segments replace the nine near-identical filter chips. Counts sit in the control so nothing has to be scanned to be counted.

Change into kit 2 min

Every task carries two to six steps with minutes on them. The first step is always small enough to do standing up.

Why the screens look like this

the ten rules
1

One thing is asked of you at a time. Today shows a single task at full size. Everything else is smaller, greyer, or folded away.

2

Time is always on screen. A live clock, a day bar, minutes on every task, a ring in Focus. Nothing says "soon".

3

Starting is pre-decided. Tasks arrive already broken into steps, so the first move is two minutes long and needs no planning.

4

Lists are capped at three. The rest are one tap away. A list you can see the end of gets started.

5

Capture never costs a modal. A single input sits on Today and inside Focus; a thought goes down in one keystroke and is sorted later.

6

Focus removes the app, not just the noise. No nav, no search, no counts — one task, one timer, one valve for intrusive thoughts.

7

Nothing shames you. No days-late counter, no broken-streak banner, streaks off by default. Late is a state, not a verdict.

8

Colour never carries meaning alone. Each state has a rail, an icon and a word, so it survives greyscale and colour blindness.

9

Charts hand you a sentence. Patterns says "Fridays are thin" in words; the bars are the evidence, not the point.

10

The room is adjustable. Motion off, spacing tight, three-at-a-time on or off — set once, applies everywhere.
